BIOMECHANICAL TRIAGE • INJURY PREVENTATIVE ARCHITECTURE

Why We Focus on Those Metrics

Most fitness trackers report cadence, stride length, and ground contact time as isolated, disconnected metrics. Runalyst connects all three to pinpoint running form: overstride is not a single number—it is the direct interaction of tempo, reach, and impact time.

THE OVERSTRIDE RISK FORMULATION
Overstride Risk = (Low Cadence) ∧ (High Stride Length relative to Speed) ∧ (Elevated GCT)
01

Low Cadence (Δ < Baseline)

When turnover drops below your personal 30-day baseline, each stride requires higher vertical flight time to sustain pace. This allows the swinging foot to drift and strike significantly ahead of your center of mass.

Biometric Signal Cadence < 30-Day Mean
02

High Stride Length / Speed

A long stride is only efficient when driven by powerful rear hip extension at race speeds. Artificially reaching forward with a straight knee during aerobic paces generates sharp deceleration shockwaves through the tibia and knee.

Biometric Signal Stride Length / Velocity Ratio ↑
03

Elevated Ground Contact Time

Spring-like running relies on rapid Achilles tendon elastic recoil (<230 ms). Landing in front of the center of mass spikes GCT (>260 ms) as the body stalls in a braking phase rather than immediately rolling forward.

Biometric Signal GCT > 250–270 ms

🧠 Corrective Mental Cues from On-Device AI Coach
“Quick off the ground”
Focus on lifting off rather than stomping down. Naturally quickens cadence and minimizes ground contact time.
“Run on ice”
Encourages light, quiet landings with soft knees to absorb impact smoothly and protect joint cartilage.
“High knees, low ankles”
Keeps lower leg tucked under the knee, preventing the foot from reaching or casting forward.
“Drive through your glutes”
Lengthens stride behind through full rear hip extension rather than overreaching out in front.
